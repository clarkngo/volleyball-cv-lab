# 2026-11-20 — Kickoff

## Why sports
- Mature areas like self-driving are crowded. Applying AI and computer vision to sports is more open.
- Goal: a **proof of concept** to support a grant / research effort.

## Approach
- **Start small:** one game, one simple rule. Publish the findings, then build on them.
- **Open source:** university-aligned research, so the baseline work is expected to be open. A service or application could come later if it proves viable.

## Sport and scope
- Discussed basketball, football and cricket. **Volleyball chosen**:
  - Fewer and less subjective challenge rules than, say, American football.
  - Video is available, including personal match footage.
- **First task:** a small, clear rule, such as ball in or out, or scoring / dead-ball detection. Generalize to other rules or sports later.
- **Coaching angle:** annotate human movement and form so players and coaches can spot inefficiencies or errors.

## Data and technical approach
- Survey existing vision models and datasets (YouTube, Kaggle, existing volleyball videos).
- Find where off-the-shelf models fall short. Decide between fine-tuning and a custom model.
- Work from video frames or APIs rather than relying only on local downloads and storage.
- Expect manual effort for labeling and tagging.

## Grant and team
- The grant needs a small core group of eligible students (about five, not counting professors).
- Expect to publish a paper or results.

## Logistics
- Meet **weekly, Fridays around 2:00 PM Seattle time** (flexible for time zones).
- Possibly twice a week or short 10–15 minute syncs once things pick up.
- A shared GitHub repo will hold project files, code and notes (the advisor creates it in the org).
